The Problem With Modern Onchain Gaming

Predatory house cuts, mempool front-running, and dishonest player abandonment

Market Inefficiencies
🎰

1. Predatory House Edges & Casino Bias

Traditional online casinos and Web3 gambling apps impose 2%–10% house edges, ensuring players are mathematically destined to lose over time. Players crave fair, skill-and-mind-game 50/50 PvP wagers without feeding an intermediary.

👀

2. Mempool Sniping & Front-Running

In naive turn-based onchain games, submitting moves unencrypted allows MEV bots, miners, or watchful opponents to inspect transactions in the mempool and counter the move before block confirmation.

🧊

3. The "Losing Player Freeze" / Griefing Bug

In typical two-player reveal games, if Player A reveals a winning move first, Player B realizes they lost and simply abandons the session—leaving the creator's stake frozen indefinitely in smart contract escrow.

🔌

4. Fragile Oracles & Vulnerable Push Transfers

Games relying on Chainlink VRF or external keepers suffer from latency and fee overhead. Furthermore, contracts that push ETH/BOT directly to external addresses can be frozen by malicious receiver fallback reverts.

PotShot Innovation

Cryptographic Commit-Reveal

Moves are permanently hidden until both players lock in. The commitment formula hashes the player's address, choice, and random 32-byte salt:

keccak256(abi.encode(player, move, salt))

Nobody can see or front-run the opponent's move.

⏰

Anti-Griefing Timeout Engine

A built-in deadline countdown ensures nobody can hold funds hostage. If a player hides their move after seeing the opponent's reveal, the honest revealer automatically wins the entire 2× pot by forfeit via claimTimeout().

Zero fees extracted by the protocol. Every win, draw, or refund credits pendingBalance. Funds exit only when the user executes withdraw(), preventing external reentrancy and blocking malicious revert griefing.

Security & Attack Resistance

Formalized defense against copy-attacks, front-running, reentrancy, and DoS

Security Architecture
🪞

Anti-Copy Attack Scheme

In naive commitment contracts, Player B can copy Player A's hash without knowing the move. PotShot embeds player in the commitment:

A copied hash creates an un-revealable commitment for Player B, resulting in guaranteed forfeiture.

🔒

Reentrancy & DoS Immunity

External calls are never made during game resolution. Funds are only transferred during an explicit withdraw() execution guarded by OpenZeppelin's ReentrancyGuard. A malicious contract that reverts on ETH receipt only harms itself.

⚡

Zero Admin Risk

No owner, no upgrade proxy, no pause switch, and no fee treasury. The contract cannot be modified, seized, or drained. The immutable code deployed to block 29,637,423 is perpetual and fully autonomous.

Game-Theoretic Dominant Strategy: Honest Reveal

Under traditional escrow, a player who realizes their move lost has an incentive to stall. Under PotShot, failing to reveal forfeits 100% of the pot to the opponent. Revealing always maintains an expected payoff ≥ forfeiting, making immediate reveal the strictly dominant strategy for all players.
